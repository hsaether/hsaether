# Hafnia Limited (HAFNI / HAFN) — IAF analysis, stub Q4-26 + 2027–2029

**v8, 18 Nov 2026** (full update after the Q3 report) · P = **NOK 90.00** (Oslo close 17 Nov) · iaf-valuation **2026-11-12.1** · Oil Shipping BBB 13 Nov · Equity BBB 6 Nov · **Verdict: fails** (Base margin of safety −9.4%).

## Summary

**The cycle first** (read from `docs/oil-shipping-bbb.md`, 13 Nov; no own rate forecast)
- **Where it is: at the peak, on a tonnage story.** Hafnia has 74% of Q4 booked at $55,148/day against $32,708 earned in Q3; its pools earned LR1 $122k, MR $76k and Handy $66k a day in the week of 9 Nov. The rally rests on fewer clean ships, not more cargo: clean-trading LR2s fell to 170 in October (−31% y/y) as they moved into dirty trade, while Middle East product exports are still 44% below February (Hafnia Q3 deck).
- **What turns it, and when (baseline Base):** the crisis structure lasts through H1-27, then LR2s return to clean trade in H2-27 and the 2028–29 deliveries land. Product rates fade from LR2 ~114 / LR1 ~69 / MR ~48 in Q1-27 to near newbuild parity in 2028–29 (LR2 38 → 35, LR1 30 → 28, MR 25 → 22; $k/day). Hafnia says the same in its own words: freight is supported "into 2027", and the risks are "dated" (the 2028–29 orderbook, crude tonnage pushing Aframaxes back into clean trade).
- **The tails:** Bear, an Iran–USA deal plus recession (earliest Q1-27) with US licences returning sanctioned tankers (~50 ships applied for, none granted), brings the fade forward and lowers mid-cycle values ~30%. Bull, US strikes or a failed Gulf of Oman STS hub plus port fees returning on 10 Jan 2027, keeps rates high into 2028.
- **What the price implies about the cycle:** at NOK 90.00 Base must earn about **19% more on every open day in 2027–29**, which puts MR 2029 near 26 against the baseline's 22, close to Bull's 27. Alternatively, exit values must sit **16% above the Base mid-cycle marks**. Either way, the price assumes a later or shallower fade than the baseline's Base. It prices ~19% of the way from the Base to the Bull hurdle.

**Verdict**
- **Fails.** Base returns about 7% a year against k = 12%. The hurdle price is **NOK 81.6 against P = NOK 90.00**, a margin of safety of **−9.4%**.
- **The dividend form does not change the verdict.** Base assumes the board's preferred special dividend: Hafnia's 20.36m TORM shares are handed to shareholders (0.0380 TORM share per Hafnia share, $1.2826 = NOK 12.34 at TORM's 17 Nov close) instead of Q3 and Q4 cash dividends. The cash fallback (Q3 90% in cash, decided by 25 Nov) gives P_k NOK 81.7.
- **Bear loses ~25% of capital** (IRR −11%, P_k NOK 52), but the balance sheet is never at risk: fully committed LTV stays ≤ 17%. **Bull** returns ~31% a year (P_k NOK 127).
- **What the price needs:** an exit value of NOK 88 against Base NOK 76 (×1.16). Base cash flows with Bull exit marks clear (17.8%).

**Base in brief**
- Base 2027 TCE is $1.26bn ($36.3k/day), in line with consensus revenue ($1.26bn; 4 estimates). Q4-26 TCE is $522m against a consensus of $455m (3 estimates, range $378–561m), carried by the booked 74%.
- **The return is cash, not value growth.** The TORM shares (NOK 12.3 in December) plus NOK 18.9 of cash dividends give d = 11.1% a year. P/NAV_0 is 1.00, so there is no re-rating, and NAV falls 5.5% a year: ships age (−NOK 12.2) and today's booked premium rolls off (−NOK 2.6), against retained cash of +NOK 9.7. **Break-even:** exit marks 16% above Base mid-cycle, open-day rates ×1.19, or both ×1.09.

**Bear in brief**
- **Story:** an Iran–USA deal plus recession ends shuttles, STS and Cape routing in H1-27; LR2s clean up and the delivery wave meets slack demand (LR2 36 / 29 / 25, MR 22 / 19 / 17). Worst year 2029: a net loss (−$13m), no dividend. Capital falls to NOK 67.4 (−25%).
- **Survival is comfortable:** fully committed LTV peaks at ~17%; liquidity ~$1.1bn (cash $300m, ~$790m undrawn) against $637m of committed newbuild and option capex. **Damage:** the Bear rate path alone takes Base 7.4% to 0.1%; Bear exit marks (~30% lower) take it to −11.0%.

## Verdict

The band rule is applied to the Base margin of safety (P_k / P − 1): clears above +2%, marginal from −2% to +2%, fails below −2%. At −9.4% the result is **fails** (see Summary).
- **What would flip it up:** a price of NOK ~80 or below; mid-cycle values re-set higher by the baseline (each +10% ≈ +NOK 5.4); a later fade, i.e. clean-trading LR2s staying low through 2027 (+12% on open days ≈ +NOK 5.2); LR1 and MR period rates holding (LR1 retest +NOK 1.0); buybacks below NAV with the retained cash.
- **What would push it further down:** the Bear catalysts (a deal plus recession, sanctioned-tanker licences, LR2s cleaning up); acquisitions at market prices with the retained cash ("other growth and consolidation opportunities").
- **What does not flip it:** the dividend form, the stub, the tail, the payout.

**Caveat:** this is a margin-of-safety screen, not an intrinsic valuation or a price target. P_k is the price at which the Base path returns exactly 12%.
