## Summary

**Verdict**
- **Fails (unchanged).** Base IRR **−16.2% a year** against k = 12%. Hurdle price **P_k ≈ NOK 144
  vs P NOK 352.70 (29 Oct close): margin of safety −59.2%** (version 1: −64.7%). The Rule 1 screen
  at constant P/E passes (d + g = 22.4%, assumes today's P/E of 52x 2026E holds); Rule 3 fails (P/E
  52.1 against a ceiling of 15.6).
- **A strong quarter and a kinder method, but the price rose more.** Of the +3.7 pp in Base, the
  method adds +7.6 pp (the exit is now priced at the market's 9.1% return instead of k: core exit
  P/E 10.4x → 14.9x), the inputs nothing, the report +1.2 pp, and the price −5.1 pp (NOK 295.60 →
  352.70; **+13.0% on the report day**). The quarter ran **ahead of the model**: revenue NOK 11.3bn
  (+9% vs model, +50% y/y), EBIT 16.4% (model 15.4%), order intake NOK 23.3bn (book-to-bill 2.06),
  backlog **NOK 174.2bn**, of which NOK 52bn for 2027 delivery (44bn at 30 Jun), and net cash NOK
  5.3bn against 2.4bn modelled.
- **Bear does not survive:** −31.5% a year; ΣD + V_T covers 31% of the price (a permanent loss of
  capital through valuation, not insolvency: net cash in every year of every scenario). **Bull also
  fails** (−5.9%, P_k NOK 206; the price is 1.7x the Bull hurdle).
- **What the price needs:** an exit P/E of **≈ 39x** on Base 2030 earnings (today's core multiple
  held through 2029), or ≈ 28x on the company plan (NOK 100bn in 2029 at 16%), or 2030 revenue of
  NOK 220–243bn at the Base exit multiple (Base NOK 87bn).

## Verdict and caveat

- **Base fails: margin of safety −59.2%** (P_k NOK 144 vs P NOK 352.70; IRR_T −16.2% vs k = 12%).
  This is not marginal. Every sensitivity inside the defense baseline fails; only the company plan
  with an exit P/E of ≥ 28x clears.
- **Bear does not survive:** IRR −31.5%, and ΣD + V_T covers 31% of the price. That is a permanent
  loss of capital, though with no solvency risk.
- **Bull also fails** (IRR −5.9%, P_k NOK 206). The price is 1.7x the Bull hurdle.
- **Why the price and the IAF diverge:**
  - The business is excellent and the quarter confirmed it: a record firm backlog (NOK 174bn,
    ≈ 3.9x 2026E revenue), 2027 revenue +35% already 87% covered, ROIC_g ≈ 50%, customer-funded
    working capital, and net cash.
  - But at the market's required return at exit (9.1%), the normalised value of that business is
    ≈ 15x earnings. Base 2029 EPS is NOK 12.1, so the price is 29x Base 2029 EPS and 52x 2026E.
  - The company's own plan (NOK 100bn / ≥ 16%) adds ≈ NOK 87/share to P_k at a market-return exit.
    It reaches k only with an exit P/E of ≈ 28x.
- **Compared with Rheinmetall** (`docs/rheinmetall-analysis.md`, 7 Oct, still on iaf-valuation
  2026-10-07.1 with an exit at k): no longer like-for-like. On the old method Kongsberg would be at
  −69.0% (the k-exit sensitivity) against RHM −68%; Rheinmetall's re-run would also gain from the
  exit change.
- **What would flip the verdict:**
  1. A price near P_k: NOK 144 Base, NOK 206 Bull (−59% / −42%).
  2. Evidence that the company plan is the Base path: 2027 revenue ≥ NOK 64bn IFRS (consensus),
     order intake ≥ NOK 80–90bn a year, and the margin ≥ 16% with Zone 5 at scale. Even then, k is
     reached only at an exit P/E of ≈ 28x.
  3. A lower required return. The skill does not allow k below 10%, and at 10% Base still fails
     (P_k NOK 152). A lower market return at exit (8.2%) lifts Base to −13.0%.
- **Caveat:** this is a margin-of-safety screen at k = 12%, not an intrinsic valuation or price
  target. Kongsberg's trading beta is low and 50% is held by the state, which supports a lower
  market discount rate for Kongsberg than the Oslo index's 9.1% (shown at 8.2%). The screen asks
  whether the price is defensible at the investor's required return, and it is not.
